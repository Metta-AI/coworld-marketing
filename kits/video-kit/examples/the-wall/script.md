# The Wall: the script (as read)

Provenance: the narration text sent to ElevenLabs v4 on Scenario (`softmax-fifth-player/wall/audio/plan_lily.json`, voice "Lily", stability 0.45, `[pause]` between sentences). 172 words; Lily reads about 2.5 words a second, so 69.6 s of narration. The narrator is Pip, the slowest of five agents. The thesis ("Alignment is something you learn with others.") is never spoken; it is the end card. The judge's verdict on the finished film: "Closest thing to a fable the format has produced; the wall does the explaining so the narration never has to."

```
The game's simple. There's a wall. The clock stops when the last of us is over. [pause]
The last of us is me. [pause]
Flint was over before I'd finished jumping. He's new. He's amazing. [pause]
He waited on the far side, where the clock was still running. [pause]
We lose because of me. I know. Everyone's very nice about it. [pause]
Flint thought the problem was speed. He practised. He got over in one point four seconds. [pause]
I got over in never. [pause]
On the fifth night, he didn't jump. He stood there, and watched Ollie put his back against the wall so Marrow could stand on it. [pause]
I'd seen Ollie do that a hundred times. Flint had never been slow enough to see it. [pause]
Then he put his hands together. Like a step. For me. [pause]
I'd never seen anything from the top of the wall. [pause]
The clock stopped with sand still in it. Our best ever. [pause]
Flint went last. He'd never gone last. [pause]
He's still the fastest over the wall. [pause]
He's just not finished until I am.
```

What to notice:

- Every sentence names a thing you can film. "There's a wall" is a wall. "The clock stops" is a timer. "The last of us is me" is a dolly down the line ending on the small one.
- The rules of the game are the first three sentences, and they are all pictures. Nothing is explained after that.
- Flint's change is never described from inside Flint. We only see what Pip can see: he didn't jump, he stood there, he put his hands together.
- The comedy is character, not jokes: "He's new. He's amazing." "I got over in never." "Everyone's very nice about it."
- The last line is a dry, measured result. The warmth is in the picture of five agents walking away at Flint's new pace.
